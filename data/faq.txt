DEPARTMENT OF JUSTICE – FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Q1. What is the Department of Justice?

The Department of Justice is a Department under the Ministry of Law and Justice, Government of India. It deals with matters relating to judicial administration, courts, judicial appointments, judicial reforms and various justice-related schemes.

Q2. What is the eCourts Project?

The eCourts Project is an initiative for using information and communication technology to improve the functioning of courts and provide digital judicial services.

Q3. What is the National Judicial Data Grid?

The National Judicial Data Grid (NJDG) is a platform providing information and data relating to cases in courts, including information about pending and disposed cases.

Q4. What are Fast Track Special Courts?

Fast Track Special Courts (FTSCs) are established for speedy disposal of cases relating to rape and offences under the Protection of Children from Sexual Offences Act.

Q5. What is Access to Justice?

Access to Justice refers to programmes and initiatives intended to improve access to legal and judicial services, particularly for people who face difficulties in accessing justice.

Q6. Where can information about judicial vacancies be found?

The Department of Justice provides information concerning judicial vacancies through its official vacancy dashboard.

Official vacancy dashboard:
https://dashboard.doj.gov.in/vacancy/

Q7. Where can citizens access eCourts services?

Citizens can access judicial services through the official eCourts portal.

https://ecourts.gov.in/

Q8. Where can citizens obtain official information from the Department of Justice?

The official Department of Justice website is:

https://doj.gov.in/

Q9. What is the purpose of the Department of Justice dashboard?

The Department of Justice dashboard provides information and statistics relating to various justice-sector programmes and initiatives, including eCourts, Fast Track Special Courts and judicial vacancies.

https://dashboard.doj.gov.in/

IMPORTANT

Information concerning laws, court procedures, vacancies and government schemes may change. Users should consult the latest official government source before relying on time-sensitive information.
