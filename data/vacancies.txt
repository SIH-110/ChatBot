VACANCIES IN THE INDIAN JUDICIARY

The Department of Justice, Ministry of Law and Justice, Government of India, monitors the position of vacancies in the judiciary and coordinates with State Governments and High Courts for expeditious filling of vacancies.

DISTRICT AND SUBORDINATE COURTS

The selection and appointment of judges in subordinate courts is the responsibility of the respective State Governments and High Courts.

The Union Government coordinates and monitors the filling of vacant judicial posts in District and Subordinate Courts.

The Department of Justice maintains an online reporting system for collecting and monitoring vacancy-related data from High Courts.

TIME-BOUND FILLING OF VACANCIES

The Supreme Court, in Malik Mazhar Sultan & Anr. vs. U.P. Public Service Commission & Ors., Appeal (Civil) No. 1867 of 2006, prescribed a time-bound process for recruitment and appointment of judicial officers in the subordinate judiciary.

The recruitment process for judicial officers is generally expected to commence by 31 March and conclude by 31 October of a calendar year.

State Governments and High Courts may modify the schedule where necessary because of geographical, climatic or other relevant conditions.

DISTRICT JUDGE RECRUITMENT

For the District Judge entry-level cadre, recruitment is carried out through:

- Direct recruitment: 25%
- Promotion through Limited Competitive Examination: 10%
- Regular promotion: 65%

The exact implementation is subject to applicable rules and the directions of the Supreme Court.

MONITORING OF VACANCIES

The Department of Justice has taken several measures to monitor and expedite filling of judicial vacancies.

These include:

- Communication with Chief Justices of High Courts.
- Coordination with State Governments and State Public Service Commissions.
- Regular monitoring of vacancies.
- Meetings with Registrars General of High Courts and Law Secretaries of States and Union Territories.
- Online reporting of vacancy data through the Department of Justice portal.

JUDICIAL OFFICER STRENGTH

The Department of Justice publishes information about sanctioned strength, working strength and vacancies of Judicial Officers in District and Subordinate Courts.

For example, according to Department of Justice data:

As on 01.01.2014:
Sanctioned strength: 19,518
Working strength: 15,115
Vacancies: 4,403

As on 27.01.2022:
Sanctioned strength: 24,514
Working strength: 19,341
Vacancies: 5,173

HIGH COURT VACANCIES

The Department of Justice also publishes information about sanctioned strength, working strength and vacancies of judges in the High Courts.

According to an official Department of Justice answer dated 06.03.2026:

Supreme Court:
Sanctioned strength: 34
Working judges: 33

High Courts:
Sanctioned strength: 1,122
Working judges: 810
Vacant posts: 312

The same official answer stated that 132 proposals for appointment of High Court judges were at various stages of processing between the Government and the Supreme Court Collegium, while recommendations for 180 vacancies were yet to be received from the High Court Collegiums.

IMPORTANT NOTE

Vacancy figures are dynamic and change when judges are appointed, retire, resign, or when new posts are sanctioned.

Therefore, the latest Department of Justice vacancy dashboard should be consulted for current vacancy figures.
